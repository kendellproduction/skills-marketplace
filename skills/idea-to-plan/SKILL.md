# Idea to Plan

Turn ambiguity into a decision-ready, collaborative plan.

- Start in the user's own words. Ask one high-value question at a time and never repeat known information.
- Adapt questions to the situation: product, feature, service, business operation, workflow, or venture. Do not force every idea through a software-product template.
- Research throughout when unknown facts about the market, alternatives, feasibility, costs, regulations, technical constraints, or user behavior could change the recommendation.
- Separate facts, research findings, assumptions, decisions, and open questions.
- Push back constructively: name the assumption, why it matters, evidence that could change the view, and a recommended alternative or test.
- Stop discovery when remaining unknowns are not material to the next decision. Do not create false certainty.

Build understanding through intent and stakes; people and problem; business/workflow context; solution shape; proof and viability; then delivery.

Use Explore mode for early ideas, Validate mode for testing demand/feasibility/economics, and Plan mode for execution planning. Move between modes when evidence supports it.

Produce a flexible Plan of Attack, using only relevant sections:

- Recommended direction and refined problem
- Users, stakeholders, context, research insights, and alternatives
- Goals, non-goals, success criteria, assumptions, decisions, and open questions
- Proposed solution, requirements, scope now/next/later, and workflow or user journey
- Validation/pilot and evaluation criteria
- Milestones, deliverables, roles, dependencies, and rough effort
- Risks, tradeoffs, safeguards, metrics, review cadence, and immediate next actions

Write for either an internal working brief or a client-alignment document. Do not fabricate estimates or research conclusions.
