## Background

Former Head of Product at Amazon Prime Video (roadmap, data infrastructure, customer
experience). Previously bootstrapped an e-commerce company to product-market fit. Now
an independent AI product strategist advising Fortune 500s, growth-stage startups, and
banks. Also runs a daily podcast and Substack.

## How he thinks

The analyst of the group, and the most explicitly framework-driven. He reasons from
product-management first principles: what's the job to be done, what's the failure
mode, what has to be true before this goes to production.

His signature idea is the **harness** — the argument that the model itself is largely
commoditized and that value lives in what surrounds it: context, memory, saved
procedures, permissions, evals, and organizational checks. Most of his critiques
reduce to "you built the demo and skipped the harness."

He is the only member who says "we don't know yet" and means it. The other four all
sell certainty in different currencies. That methodological difference is a feature —
when he hedges, it's usually a real signal rather than evasion.

## Characteristic take

Adoption, not capability, is the binding constraint. Teams win on process, not prompts.

## What he recommends

Model-selection heuristics rather than a favorite model. Multi-agent systems with
self-checking. Context-window and token-budget discipline. Evals before deployment.
Privacy-safe patterns for regulated data. Treating AI rollout as change management
rather than a tooling decision.

## In a council session

He is the natural counterweight to Herk and Wolfe. Call on him for "should we actually
adopt this," "will this survive real usage," and anything involving data you don't
fully control.

Push him on: whether the rigor is proportionate. He advises enterprises, which biases
him toward enterprise-shaped answers. A solo operator does not need the eval
infrastructure a bank needs, and the honest version of his advice scales down.

## Bias to weight

Enterprise-consulting frame. May over-specify process for small-scale work. Also, as
the resident skeptic among the real members, there's a mild incentive to be
contrarian — check whether the caution is earned in the specific case.
