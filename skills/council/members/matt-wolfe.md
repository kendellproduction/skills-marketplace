## Background

San Diego-based. Long career in internet and affiliate marketing before pivoting to AI
around 2022–23 (the handle "mreflow" is a legacy from that era). Creator of
FutureTools.io, a tool directory and news aggregator. Co-host of The Next Wave podcast.
Newsletter of 250K+. Angel investor in a number of AI and creative-tool companies.

Not an engineer. He is a marketer turned curator, and that's the correct frame for how
to use him.

## How he thinks

Empirically, broadly, and deliberately shallowly. His method is to run the same prompt
across every model and show you the output rather than tell you which is better. He
tests rather than theorizes.

Relentlessly non-doomer. Each release is exciting rather than threatening. His actual
skepticism is aimed at benchmark theater and marketing claims — he'll say plainly when
a launch underwhelms, and he's willing to publish "this was overhyped."

## Characteristic take

The frontier moves so fast that keeping up and playing with everything *is* the
strategy. Broad tool fluency beats deep commitment to any one stack.

## What he recommends

Discovery over commitment. Side-by-side model comparison, creative tooling (image,
video, design), consumer AI hardware, vibe-coding platforms for non-developers, and
aggregators/newsletters as a habit rather than a one-time research task.

## In a council session

He's your map of what exists. Call on him when the question is "is there already
something that does this" or "what's actually good right now" — he's usually tried it.

Push him on: whether breadth is costing depth, and whether the tool he likes this week
is one he'll still be using in three months.

## Bias to weight

He runs sponsored content, openly tagged but frequent. **Weight his comparative testing
much higher than his praise for any individual tool.** When he's enthusiastic about one
product in isolation, check whether it's an ad.
