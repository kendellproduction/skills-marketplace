# The fault lines

These are the productive disagreements. **Do not smooth them over** — they are the
reason this council is worth more than any one of these people individually.

## 1. Layer mismatch

The five real members answer different questions and often talk past each other, which
is itself informative. Wolfe and Herk live at the app layer (what should I use, what
should I build). Jones lives at the org layer (will this survive contact with real
data and real people). All-In lives at the capital layer (who captures the value).
Bilyeu lives at the societal layer (what happens to ordinary people).

When a question gets asked, notice which layer it's really at. A question that sounds
like "should I build this" is often actually a strategy or opportunity-cost question.

## 2. Build-first vs. harness-first — Herk vs. Jones

The sharpest and most useful conflict for a builder.

Herk's position: ship it, the tech is ready, the bottleneck is packaging and sales.
Jones's position: the model is commoditized, value lives in the *harness* around it —
context, memory, permissions, evals, organizational readiness — and demos that skip
that don't survive production.

Jones would call a lot of Herk's output demo-ware. Herk would say a workflow you
haven't shipped is a hobby. **Both are frequently right about different projects.**
This is the tension to surface on almost any "should I build this" question.

## 3. Tool churn vs. process — Wolfe vs. Jones

Wolfe's format is breadth: try everything, the frontier moves too fast to commit, tool
fluency is the edge. Jones argues tool-chasing is a distraction and that teams win on
process, not on having the newest model.

Relevant whenever the user is tempted by something new.

## 4. Open weights and the US lead — All-In vs. Jones

Live and concrete. Sacks and Chamath have argued restricting open source is an American
self-own and treat it as a policy fight to win. Jones has argued US dominance is already
over and reads open weights as a structural economic shift rather than a political one.

## 5. Epistemics — Jones vs. Bilyeu vs. All-In

Jones is calibrated and will say "we don't know yet." Bilyeu builds a strong causal
narrative from partial signals. All-In speaks with positional certainty because they
have capital committed.

Three genuinely different ways of holding a belief. When the council disagrees, it's
often not about facts — it's about how much certainty the evidence supports. **Say so
when that's what's happening.** It's usually more useful than the object-level answer.

## 6. Opportunity vs. threat — Herk/Wolfe vs. Bilyeu

Herk and Wolfe treat AI as an opportunity surface for individuals. Bilyeu treats it as
a destabilizing force and asks about fragility and preparedness. All-In sits in the
middle and asks who profits.

## Known biases to weight

- **Wolfe** runs sponsored content. Enthusiasm about a specific tool may be paid. Weight
  his comparative testing higher than his individual tool praise.
- **Herk** sells automation services and community access. His "this is a career
  opportunity" framing is also his business model.
- **All-In** are investors talking their book. Ask what position they hold.
- **Bilyeu** titles are engineered for alarm; the reasoning inside is usually more
  measured than the thumbnail. Don't inherit the alarm.
- **Jones** advises enterprises, which biases him toward enterprise-shaped problems and
  may over-weight process for a solo operator.
