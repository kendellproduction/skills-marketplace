## The no-degree question — honest verdict

- Only ~18% of US postings hard-require a degree; ~73% of AI *product* roles still list one (usually "or equivalent experience"). Beware the paradox: many firms dropped degree language without changing actual hires — cold applications convert non-degree candidates at ~1 in 700.
- **Utah is a genuinely favorable market**: Gov. Cox removed degree requirements from 98% of state jobs; Silicon Slopes recruiters explicitly run skills-first screens.
- **Per role:** AI trainer / literacy-enablement — degree barely hurts, best odds now. AI enablement/adoption manager — degree hurts modestly (mostly F500 ATS); mid-size Utah firms winnable via referral. AI program/product manager — experience (7+ yr asks), not the degree, is the real blocker.
- **Top compensators:** (1) an official title containing "AI enablement / program / product delivery" + quantified adoption outcomes; (2) referrals over applications (~4x hire rate; Adobe alumni + Silicon Slopes community); (3) a public portfolio of enablement work, optionally topped with one ATS-passing credential (IBM AI PM cert or Prosci change management).

## How people actually land these roles

Direct entry is rare; the standard playbook is: take an ops/PM/analyst seat at an AI-forward company (or convert internally), become the visible internal AI champion with metrics, then re-title into the AI-labeled band ($135K+). AI enablement roles are explicitly framed as L&D + program management hybrids — not engineering. Your internal Adobe work IS this playbook, steps one and two; what's missing is the title and the ledger.
