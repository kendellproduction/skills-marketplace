# The Council's Ruling

## 1. The Verdict: Parallel — but a specific, sequenced parallel, with external as the default

All five advisors voted "parallel," which the cross-examiner rightly flagged as herding. But when you weigh the *effective* votes, the picture sharpens: Advisors 1, 2, and 4 argued for parallel-with-external-as-forcing-function — which is really "external prep, with one internal ask on a clock." Advisor 5's bull case for staying rested partly on signals generated at a price of zero (free training demand proves nothing about willingness to pay), and Advisor 1's strongest internal-pessimism fact — that Adobe already runs a centralized AI enablement machine that would absorb you — could not be verified and may be invented. Discarding the inflated comp figures ($208K medians, unverified posting counts) and the false regulatory claims (the EU AI Act literacy deadline passed 18 months ago and is being *softened*; the DOL framework is voluntary guidance, not a mandate), here is what survives: the roles you'd fit exist and hire non-engineers; they realistically pay ~$130-180K, not $150-230K; Adobe is genuinely in cost-discipline mode with a CEO transition; and the true first-hop money delta is $10-40K, not $40-70K.

So the ruling is **parallel, correctly understood**: the internal and external tracks are not competitors for your hours — they *stack*, because they share one prerequisite. Your binding constraint is not skills, not the window, not even Adobe's politics. It is the words "Systems Analyst" on your resume. External screens will reject you on title before any human reads about your trainings and tools, and the real competitive flood is not your newly-AI-literate coworkers rising from below — it's displaced, formally-titled PMs and transformation managers rebranding into "AI transformation lead" from above, in a market where the job-switcher premium is at a record low. The internal retitle (Product Delivery Manager alignment, or an AI program title) is cheap for a manager to grant — no new headcount, no new budget — and it reprices every future move. It is worth more than a raise. That makes the internal ask a *prerequisite* for the external track, not a sentimental hedge.

And the external track's first job is not leverage or exit — it's **falsification**. Every advisor assumed your PdM and enablement work clears an external bar; nobody tested it. Ten real applications with an outcomes-rewritten resume is the only honest test. If they produce screens, you have market pricing and options. If they produce silence, that's decision-grade information that your evidence file — not the market — is the problem, and you fix it while still employed. The one path the council unanimously rejects, and the ruling affirms: waiting for Adobe to create a role around you. A company down ~36% YTD, running PIP-driven quiet cuts, mid-CEO-transition, whose AI story to Wall Street is Firefly revenue — that company does not mint bespoke cost-center roles for mid-level analysts. It consumes free labor. It is consuming yours right now.

## 2. The Decisive Verified Facts

1. **Adobe is in confirmed cost-discipline and leadership transition.** Narayen announced his exit March 12, 2026 (managed transition — he stays until a successor lands and remains Chair); stock down ~36% YTD by mid-2026; rolling PIP-driven attrition of an estimated 200-400/quarter with no backfill, concentrated in Digital Experience and engineering. New internal role creation in this environment is slow, political, and rare.
2. **The AI-skills premium is real and still rising, but it accrues to *demonstrated, embedded* skill.** PwC's 2026 Barometer (1B+ postings): 62% wage premium, up from 57%; AI-skilled postings up ~140% YoY. Meanwhile standalone "prompt engineer" postings fell 30-40% in two years — generic fluency commoditizes, documented adoption outcomes don't.
3. **The roles you'd target exist and hire non-engineers, at ~$130-180K.** Glassdoor puts AI enablement at a $138K average ($106-181K range); AI PM averages $159K (ZipRecruiter) to $198K (Glassdoor). Postings favor change-management, program delivery, and evidence portfolios over CS degrees. This is real — but it's the honest band, not the $208K fantasy.
4. **The adoption gap — your actual market — is wide open.** Adobe's own 2026 Digital Trends research: a majority of enterprises have no active agentic AI use; under a quarter are even piloting. Only ~17% of employees use AI frequently. The enabler's job is not close to done.
5. **Generic job-switching no longer pays.** ADP data shows the switcher premium at a record-low ~2 points. The only move that pays in 2026 is a *role and level* jump — which is exactly why the title, not the salary, is your first battle.

## 3. Is the Window Real?

Yes — but it's a different window than the one you fear, and the timeline is more forgiving on one side and less forgiving on the other.

The window you're worried about — "I know AI better than non-technical peers" — is real and short: roughly **12-24 months** before baseline AI literacy is table stakes at any serious employer (Meta already ties reviews to AI usage; vendor tooling trains users itself). But note: that edge was never your asset. It's the depreciating wrapper around your asset. The claim that regulation would accelerate this (EU AI Act, DOL mandates) did not survive fact-checking — the commoditization is coming from employers and vendors, not law, which slows it slightly.

The window that should actually drive your urgency is the **title window**: as "AI enablement/transformation" roles formalize, they're being filled by displaced PMs and transformation managers with worse practical AI knowledge and better resumes than yours. Every quarter you remain "Systems Analyst" on paper, your lived experience gets harder to sell against their paper. That window is closing now, and it closes screen by screen, not all at once.

What does *not* decay — what compounds — is a quantified record of making an enterprise actually adopt AI: teams trained, tools shipped, hours saved, with your name and a title attached. You are currently generating this asset every week and capturing none of it. The correct response to the window is not panic-learning more AI. It's **conversion**: turn reputation into title, title into market position, before the formalization wave finishes.

## 4. The Pay Problem

You are underpaid — but by roughly half what the council first claimed, and that changes the tactics. The advisors compared national-average analyst pay to top-of-market AI PM pay. Realistically: Adobe likely pays you $110-140K total comp; your first external hop lands in the $130-180K band. True first-move delta: **$10-40K**, possibly less against unvested RSUs. Real money, not run-through-a-wall money.

What that means:

- **The gap is a title problem wearing a salary costume.** A 3-8% internal raise is band-capped and forgettable. The PdM/AI-program *title* is what reprices your next move by $40K+. Ask for the title first, explicitly, as role-alignment: "My scope matches the PdM profile plus AI program leadership; I want the title and band to match." Frame it on evidence, not grievance.
- **Do not brandish an external offer at Adobe.** That's 2021 advice. At a company culling through PIPs, surfacing an offer is as likely to move you up the quiet-cut list as up the pay band. An external offer in 2026 Adobe is an *exit instrument*, not a negotiation chip. Never accept a counteroffer that fixes pay but not title.
- **Stop expanding the free third job.** Fulfill existing training commitments, but route every *new* team request through your manager as demand evidence: "A fourth org asked this month — this is a full-time function." Don't withhold work you've promised (illegibility is dangerous during a culling), but stop donating new scope. Free labor is subsidizing Adobe's option to never create the role.

## 5. Your 90-Day Plan

Honest budget: you have maybe **5-8 discretionary hours/week**. The council's combined wish list was a burnout plan. This is the pruned version. Explicitly cut: Prosci/PMP certifications (60-100 hour purchases; buy one only if an actual rejection cites the gap), generic AI upskilling (it's the depreciating asset), a newsletter, and any new free training commitments.

**Days 1-21 — The Evidence Ledger (~10 hours total).**
One document. Every AI contribution quantified: teams trained, session counts, attendance, tools shipped, users, hours saved, PdM deliverables owned — mapped against Adobe's own PdM job-architecture profile. This single artifact is simultaneously your internal business case, your resume rewrite, and your interview script. Nothing else starts until it exists.

**Days 21-45 — The Internal Ask (~4 hours prep, one meeting, one follow-up).**
Take a one-page version to your manager, then skip-level: formal role-alignment to PdM (or AI Program Lead) — title and band — with the AI enablement charter attached. Ask for a decision timeline of ~60 days, in writing. A written no is valuable. Silence past the deadline is a no. Do not present this as a threat; present it as housekeeping — the org chart catching up to reality.

**Days 21-60 — The Rewrite and the Falsification Test (~2-3 hours/week).**
Rewrite resume and LinkedIn around outcomes, never leading with "Systems Analyst": "Led AI enablement across N teams at Adobe; built internal tools adopted by X users; ran product delivery for Y." Then submit **10 real applications** to AI program manager / AI PM / AI enablement roles in the honest $130-180K band. This is not calibration theater — it's the test of the premise every advisor skipped. Track screen rate.

**Days 45-90 — Read the Signal, Ship One Proof (~3 hours/week).**
- If applications produce screens: interview. You're building pricing data and live options.
- If ten applications produce zero screens: the problem is packaging. Fix the ledger's numbers, get one named internal sponsor to be a reference, and consider whether the internal title just became *more* urgent, not less.
- Either way: attach a hard number to one existing internal tool ("saved X hours across Y teams") and get one executive from a team that requested your training to know about it by name. One tool with a number beats ten demos.
- Post 1-2 sanitized public artifacts on LinkedIn about the adoption program you built. Two, not four. Proof, not content marketing.

**Day 90 — The Decision Gate (pick one date; this is it: ~November 10, 2026).**
Pre-committed rule: if Adobe has not delivered at least a title change or a written commitment with a date, the internal track is demoted to background and external becomes primary — full pipeline, interviews to offer. Tell one specific person (partner, friend, mentor) the date and the rule today. Your revealed preference for three years has been to absorb scope and wait; the deadline only works if someone else holds it.

## 6. What Would Change the Answer

**Flip to internal-primary if:**
- The incoming CEO's early agenda includes a named internal AI transformation mandate with budget and an owner. New executives need cheap early wins, and your pre-built, metered adoption program is the cheapest win in the building. If this lands, re-pitch the owner within two weeks with your ledger. (Counterweight: watch where the money actually goes — if it all flows to Firefly and product monetization, the mandate is rhetorical and the flip is off.)
- Adobe grants the title and band this quarter. Then bank it, keep performing, and revisit externally in 12 months from a far stronger position.

**Flip to external-immediately if:**
- PIP waves or reorgs reach your org or your manager's chain — at that point positioning gives way to self-preservation.
- The internal ask is met with vague deferral twice ("after the reorg," "next cycle"). Two deferrals equal a no.
- You get an external offer at $150K+ with a real AI program/PM title. At the honest math, that offer *is* the retitle plus the raise Adobe won't give — take it and don't look back for a counter.

**Re-diagnose (don't flip, fix) if:**
- Ten well-targeted applications yield zero screens. That means the market can't see what you've done — the fix is evidence and framing, possibly one targeted credential, not a different strategy.

**Slow down (slightly) if:**
- "AI enablement" postings begin visibly collapsing into general PM/ops roles the way prompt engineering did. That's the signal to target **AI product/program roles with P&L adjacency** rather than pure enablement titles — treat enablement as your portfolio, not your identity. Early indicators suggest planning for this anyway: the enablement title may be a 2-4 year bridge, and the durable landing has always been the titled product role where AI fluency is the differentiator, not the job.

The single most probable failure mode of this entire exercise is that nothing changes — you keep doing three jobs for one salary, competently and invisibly, while the title window closes. Every advisor named that risk. Only the deadline defeats it.
